Three items cannot follow the code as written

The rear setback is unavoidable: the R-2 setbacks leave about 396 sq ft of buildable area on this lot. The garage width and the parking count pull against each other: two cars would meet the 65 % limit, but a duplex needs three spaces. The front setback may be settled by the neighbours' building line.

Do not demolish before the Board of Zoning Appeals decides. Tearing the house down first forfeits its grandfathered setbacks (section 1373.06).

Structure in brief

Concrete walls 12 in. thick hold back 8 to 10.5 ft of soil on three sides and are propped at the top by the Unit A floor. The garage front is open, so a steel beam on four steel columns carries the front of both floors and the roof and resists sideways load. That frame is the critical engineered element. Floors are 20 in. open-web wood trusses spanning front to back, which keeps the garage free of columns.

Every member size on the structural sheets is a stated assumption for the engineer to check against a soil report.

Confirm before relying on it

  • Drain fall. The French drain has only about 6 inches of fall to the street. The survey must confirm it works by gravity; otherwise the choices are a pump or a higher garage floor.
  • Parking is for occupants. The zoning code reserves the three required spaces for the building's occupants. Renting them to outsiders needs the Planning Division's answer.
  • Common lighting. It runs on Unit A's meter and should be disclosed in that lease.
  • Renting by the bedroom can be classed as a rooming house, a different use from a duplex.

  • Structural engineer. Retaining walls, the front steel frame, shoring beside 441 and 442 Baird and 386 Forest.
  • Civil engineer and City Engineer. Driveway on an 11 % one-lane street, the gravity drain outlet at the street edge, sidewalk waiver.
  • Utilities. Sewer and water main locations and depths from MUB; service type and meter location from Mon Power.
